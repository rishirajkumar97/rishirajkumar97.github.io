01 / The question

Can a network find its own balance?

Biological neural networks maintain useful activity despite constantly changing conditions. This project investigated whether homeostatic plasticity could bring that same self-regulating behaviour to a computational cortical microcircuit.

The goal was to explore mechanisms that guide firing rates towards configurable targets through local adjustment, rather than relying entirely on externally fixed parameters.

02 / The approach

Model, perturb, observe.

Using the Potjans–Diesmann cortical microcircuit as a foundation, I built and ran computational experiments in NEST. Python, NumPy, pandas, and Matplotlib supported the experiment workflow and analysis of spike-rate dynamics across network populations.

The work focused on how homeostatic adjustments influence network stability and activity over time, connecting a neuroscience hypothesis with a reproducible computational investigation.

03 / Why it matters

Learning from adaptive systems.

Homeostatic mechanisms offer a compelling direction for spiking neural networks: systems that can adapt while preserving stable behaviour. This research strengthened my interest in the intersection of neuroscience, machine learning, and resilient system design.

The project was conducted as academic research; it does not claim a production-ready learning rule or clinical application.
